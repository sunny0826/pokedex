# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Pokemon fans and game players, with mobile as the primary use context. They come to browse Pokemon quickly, compare details, filter by type or generation, save favorites, simulate battles, and build teams without leaving the feeling of using a handheld Pokedex.

## Product Purpose

This product is an interactive Pokedex experience, not a website-style information page. It should behave like a compact Pokemon tool: fast to open, easy to scan, tactile to use, and useful for exploration, collection, battle reference, and team planning.

Success means users feel they are operating a Pokedex rather than reading a generic web page with Pokemon colors.

## Brand Personality

Pokedex-like, Pokemon-native, player-focused.

The tone should feel playful and game-aware, but still clear enough for repeated lookup tasks on small screens.

## Anti-references

Do not make the product feel like a marketing landing page, SaaS dashboard, generic content site, blog, or decorative fan page.

Avoid layouts that simply wrap ordinary web UI in Pokemon colors. Avoid oversized hero sections, website-style feature explanations, and desktop-first compositions that make the Pokedex feel like a themed page instead of a usable device.

## Design Principles

1. Treat the Pokedex as the product surface, not a decorative container.
2. Prioritize mobile touch ergonomics for browsing, filtering, collecting, and comparing.
3. Preserve game familiarity through tactile controls, compact data, and Pokemon-native affordances.
4. Keep information dense but readable, with clear hierarchy for quick lookup.
5. Let playful motion and visual detail support interaction instead of distracting from it.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA where practical. Maintain readable text on mobile screens, touch targets that are comfortable to hit, keyboard-visible focus states for interactive controls, semantic labels for icon buttons, and reduced-motion fallbacks for repeated animations or transitions.
